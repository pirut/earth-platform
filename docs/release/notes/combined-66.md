Earth Platform build 66: no more grey cliffs along the Amazon's rivers.

Needs Minecraft 1.21.1 and NeoForge 21.1.256 or newer. For shaders, add Iris 1.8 and Sodium 0.8 and a Voxy-capable pack such as Photon v1.3b. Give the game about 6 GB of memory with shaders.

New since build 65:
- Forested river banks no longer stand over the water in sheer grey stone walls, as all along the Amazon, where the real banks are a few metres high. Outside the United States and a few other countries the elevation data reads the top of a forest's canopy as the ground, so wherever forest meets a river or the sea the land stood 12 to 26 blocks over the water in a cliff, near and far alike; from a distance it showed as a grey band with pale stripes under the trees. Banks were already eased down to the water up to 14 blocks high; forested banks now ease from up to 24 blocks higher still, so the forest slopes down to the water instead. Grass and clearings among the trees ease with them, rather than standing up as pillars. Mountain valleys keep their shores as before. Ground already generated near you keeps its old banks; ground generated from now on, and all far terrain, gets the new ones.
- Replay Mod recordings now show you while you ride the jet stream. Before, a replay drew the gliding player about three and a half ticks behind the recorded path (some 200 blocks at jet speed), and only within about 38 blocks of the camera, so a camera following the path never saw them. Other players' gliders in multiplayer keep up the same way. In a replay a fast glider is now drawn about 1.5 ticks (77 ms) behind its recorded path.
- Under shaders, the far ocean no longer shows pale patches where the water went missing in rows of far cells. Far water's floor now sits behind the water's surface as the game's own coarse depth holds it, so packs measure the same water depth on every row.
- A firework rocket can no longer freeze the game. A rocket that rose out of the ground the server had loaded around you, such as one left behind when a rocket climb outran it, was held still there forever and never exploded, so it was saved with the world. Your game kept flying it on, faster every tick, until working out what it might hit took so long the game stopped, and it did so again each time you came back. Rockets now burn out on time wherever they are, and in the Earth a runaway rocket can't fly faster than 16 blocks a tick.
- A map and compass for survival. Players who can't use the travel map now get a map on **N** too: the same globe, centered on you, that never moves anyone. Click a spot, a famous place or another player and press **Point compass here**, and a compass you carry points there; one pointed at a player follows them. With a compass in hand the action bar says what it points to and how far away that is, as in "Mount Fuji, Japan · 8,555 km". `/earth compass` does the same from chat (`to`, `place`, `here`, `player`, `home`, and `spawn` to make it plain again). Server operators can switch it off with `/earth settings survivalMap off`.
- A plain compass on the Earth now points to your spawn. Before, it pointed to 0° N 0° E, in the Gulf of Guinea off West Africa, wherever you were. A recovery compass also shows how far away you died. During a Where on Earth round every compass spins and shows no distance, so it can't give the answer away.
