Earth Platform build 65: far terrain draws faster, and stays clean when you zoom in.

Needs Minecraft 1.21.1 and NeoForge 21.1.256 or newer. For shaders, add Iris 1.8 and Sodium 0.8 and a Voxy-capable pack such as Photon v1.3b. Give the game about 6 GB of memory with shaders.

New since build 64:
- Zooming in on far terrain, with a spyglass or a zoom mod, no longer makes its blocks smear into streaks, poke through each other and flicker. Far faces are drawn a hair past their edges so no sky shows through the seams, and that hair was a fixed share of the distance: half a pixel normally, but several pixels when zoomed. It is now half a pixel on screen at any zoom. The normal view looks the same, and it costs nothing in speed.
- Shallow seas no longer glow in pale patches. Sea pickles, which give off light, were scattered over every warm and lukewarm sea bed, so the bed under shallow water showed bright blotches around them. As in vanilla, they now grow only in warm seas, in a few clusters. This applies to sea beds generated from now on.
- The sea bed no longer shows pale squares after you arrive somewhere. When the height data of a 16-block column reached the game after the blocks beside it were already drawn, those blocks kept light worked out as if that column were open sky, so under water they stayed lit as if no water were above them. They are now drawn again once it arrives.
- Far terrain draws faster, and looks the same. On Windows with an RTX 2080 Ti and a Ryzen 9 3900X (1920 x 1080, Sharpest far detail, over Yosemite Valley), stock Photon went from 65 to 80 fps standing still and from 37 to 42 fps flying; without shaders, from 78 to 201 fps standing still and from 39 to 54 fps flying. With shaders, far terrain wholly hidden behind nearer far terrain is no longer sent through the pack's shading at all, and each far face's texture and colour are looked up once per pixel rather than once per corner. Without shaders, the cloud deck no longer goes over every far column every frame to keep clear of mountains, which took most of the game's own time. Standing still, far terrain no longer redoes its drawing work every frame, and picking which far terrain to draw while moving touches far less memory. Macs weren't measured; the hidden-terrain part needs OpenGL 4.3, so it stays off there.
- Replay Mod recordings open again after the player has died high up. A death above height 2047, on any big mountain, made every later recording of that world restart its loading screen every few seconds and never open. The join packet carries the spot of your last death, and our wider height format for it was lost when Replay Mod reads that packet. The game now sends that spot in the vanilla form, clamped to height 2047; the recovery compass only uses its direction, so it points the same way.
- With shaders, shallow water no longer shows a lighter disk around you with a hard edge against darker far water (clearest from above over the Bahamas banks or a fjord's shore). The pack judges how deep water is from what lies under it, and far water was always given at least 24 blocks of depth, while the same water up close showed its real bed. Far water now has its real bed under it too, so it reads the same as near water; past a couple of kilometres it reads deep as before. Without shaders nothing changes.
- /place works in the Earth. Every /place (structure, feature, jigsaw, template) said "That position is not loaded", even right beside you: vanilla checks each chunk at height 0, which on the Earth is rock far under the ground and never loaded. A place now counts as loaded when the ground there is, and /place structure sets villages, outposts and the rest on the real ground at any height, as natural ones are, instead of the old 16-block slice of world that cut them off.
- Every speed change has a switch to turn it off; see docs/FLAGS.md and docs/perf/build-65-speed.md.
