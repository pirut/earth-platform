Earth Platform build 70: Minecraft's own caves in new worlds, nearby blocks hidden behind rock no longer drawn, and far terrain does less work on your processor while you fly.

Needs Minecraft 1.21.1 and either NeoForge 21.1.256 or newer, or Fabric Loader 0.16.14 or newer with Fabric API. For shaders, add Iris 1.8 and Sodium 0.8 and a Voxy-capable pack such as Photon v1.3b. Give the game about 6 GB of memory with shaders.

New since build 69:
- New worlds carve their caves with Minecraft's own cave code: cheese caverns with stone pillars, winding spaghetti tunnels, thin noodle caves and cave entrances, the same shapes and sizes as a vanilla world, worked out by the same density functions and noises vanilla uses, seeded as vanilla seeds them. Before, Earth had its own simpler caves: smooth blobs and tunnels about 10 blocks wide, everywhere at every depth, so caves felt too big and too samey. The caves follow the ground as the rock layers do, so they sit at vanilla's depths under each valley or seabed. Worlds made before build 70 keep their old caves, so new ground meets saved ground without cut-off caves. Underground cubes take about 0.3 ms more each to make.
- Flying no longer makes Earth recheck every far column each frame. Before, each time the camera moved or turned even slightly, Earth worked out again which of tens of thousands of far columns were in view, and queued them all to draw. Now it picks the columns that could show from anywhere within 16 blocks of the camera, in a slightly wider view, and keeps that pick while you stay inside it. Columns that can't be seen draw nothing, so the picture is the same. On our test PC (RTX 2080 Ti, Photon, Sharpest settings, flying at the Yosemite vista) far terrain's work on the render thread dropped from about 4.1 ms to 1.6 ms a frame.
- Far lakes, rivers and sea are drawn in large batches instead of one far column at a time, in each of the water passes. Far water's work on the render thread dropped from about 1.2 ms to 0.9 ms a frame on the same test.
- On that PC the frame rate stayed the same, about 78 fps standing and flying, because the graphics card was the limit there. Computers with a slower processor, or playing without shaders, should see more of the saving.
- Both can be switched off with `-Dearthplatform.far.viewMargin=false` and `-Dearthplatform.far.batchWater=false` (see docs/FLAGS.md).
- New worlds take rock, red ground, soil, plants and tree heights from data instead of boxes of latitude and longitude. Before, Earth kept a short list of boxes: red sand between these longitudes in Australia, lava fields in six named places, bamboo and cherries inside a rectangle over Asia, cacti in a strip over the Americas, and giant sequoias in fifteen listed groves. Worlds made before this build keep those boxes, so their new ground matches the old.
- Trees stand as tall as the real forest. Earth reads Meta and the World Resources Institute's canopy height map (downloaded for the places you visit, like land cover) and each tree picks the shape nearest that height, with extra trunk under its crown where the forest is taller. In our comparison the average crown rose from 19 to 25 blocks in the Amazon, from 5 to 11 in the Black Forest and from 6 to 12 on Mount Rainier's slopes, and stayed at 8 in Finland's and Yakutia's taiga.
- Giant trees grow wherever the map shows conifers 45 m and taller standing together, not only in the Sierra Nevada's listed groves: the coast redwoods, the Hoh rainforest and the old forests around Mount Rainier now have them. The Sierra groves follow the map too, so Giant Forest and Mariposa Grove are whole groves among ordinary forest rather than one 2 km disc. Yosemite Valley and Lake Tahoe still have none.
- Conifer country whose trees stand 33 m and taller is old-growth conifer forest whatever the climate alone would grow there, so the Hoh and Mount Rainier's lower slopes are big spruces and pines instead of oak and birch, and the giants stand among them.
- Spruce and pine crowns in new worlds are filled where vanilla leaves the trunk bare between tiers and on top, so a conifer forest seen from above is green rather than dotted with brown log ends.
- New giant trees in new worlds, in three forms chosen by climate. Giant sequoias (under 1,500 mm of rain a year, as in the Sierra Nevada) are fluted red columns on flared feet under a dome of heavy upswept limbs, each ending in a billow of foliage. Coast redwoods (wetter, and 10.5 C or warmer over the year) and giant firs (wetter and cooler, as in the Hoh rainforest and on Mount Rainier) are tapering spires of drooping boughs with flat pads of foliage, in red bark and dark. Worlds made before this build keep their sequoias. On far terrain a thick trunk inside its crown is drawn as crown, and the most distant giants as dark spires, in every world.
- Lava country and granite country follow the Global Lithological Map (shipped in the mod as a small map, nothing to download). Black lava fields now show wherever the bedrock is young basalt and little grows, such as Idaho's Craters of the Moon, and granite such as Half Dome is paler rock. Hawaii, Iceland, Etna, the Galápagos and Réunion look the same as before.
- Red ground follows how red the land is from orbit, from NASA's Blue Marble (also shipped in the mod). The Namib, the Simpson Desert and the Pilbara are red, the Sahara's and Arabia's sands are orange only as far as they are from orbit (about a quarter in the Libyan Desert, next to none in the Great Sand Sea), and red country ends in tongues and patches instead of along a straight line.
- Northern forest floors have more podzol where SoilGrids shows deep humus, as across Finland.
- Cacti grow in the Americas' realms, bamboo in tropical Asia and the warm wet parts of East Asia, and cherries in East Asia's monsoon and humid climates, by ecoregion realm and climate rather than rectangles.
- New worlds can leave any of these out with `-Dearthplatform.earth.canopy=false`, `-Dearthplatform.earth.maps=false` and `-Dearthplatform.earth.realms=false` (see docs/FLAGS.md). The mod's file grows by about 0.9 MB.
- Nearby blocks hidden behind rock are no longer drawn. Each nearby cube now notes which of its sides its open space joins, and Earth draws only the cubes the camera could see into through that open space, the way Minecraft culls its own chunk sections (it never did this for Earth's cubes before). Standing on the Yosemite valley floor, it draws 74 nearby cubes instead of 211; in a cave room, 155 instead of 338. Chests, signs and other block entities in those hidden cubes are skipped too (a beacon's beam still shows). The picture is the same. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.caveCull=false`.
- With a shader pack, nearby terrain that can't cast a shadow into your view is left out of the shadow map, as Iris already does for Minecraft's own chunks (and only where the pack allows it). Under Photon on the Yosemite valley floor the shadow map drew 211 nearby cubes instead of 1,335. The picture is the same. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.shadowCull=false`. And deep in a cave with no way out in sight, the shadow map takes only the rock that could shade that cave: 171 cubes instead of 536 in a sealed test room.
- Far terrain is now kept on disk with the world. Before, every far column was made again each time a world opened, even with all the map data already downloaded: about 17,000 columns for one view at the default distance, some 40 seconds of every processor core at Mount Shasta. Now a column made once is saved in the world's `far` folder and read back the next time, so the far view of a place you have been returns far sooner after a restart, and on a server every player shares what any of them has seen. The columns are made again after an update, when mods that add blocks or biomes change, and wherever the ground under them is changed. At most 2 GB is kept per world, the least recently used going first. Off with `-Dearthplatform.far.store=false`.
- Far terrain that is only ground, water and forest cover (most of it from 8-block voxels outward) is now made, sent and saved as a short list of vertical runs for each voxel column, instead of every voxel filled in. The client already drew such columns from runs, after working the runs out again from the voxels it was sent; now the server makes them once and nothing is filled in or worked out twice. Columns with trees drawn as trees, sequoia groves, layered canyon rock or your builds are sent as before. The picture is the same, and tests compare the two forms voxel for voxel. Off with `-Dearthplatform.far.surfacePackets=false`; `-Dearthplatform.far.checkPackets=true` makes every such column both ways and logs any that differ.
- A server now holds at most 4,096 far columns waiting for each player. The game itself asks for a few hundred at a time; a changed client could ask for over a million and keep the server's far terrain workers busy for hours.
- Save files are forced onto the disk before they replace the old ones, so a power cut or crash while saving can no longer leave an empty file where a region's edits were.
- Nearby terrain is drawn in batches. Each nearby cube used to have its own buffers and its own draw call for every layer, in the main view and again in a shader pack's shadow map. Now cubes share buffers by region (8 cubes a side) and each layer of a region draws in one call. On the Yosemite valley floor under Photon, nearby terrain took 240 draw calls a frame instead of 1,712; looking down on the forest, 712 instead of 3,550. On our test PC (RTX 2080 Ti, Photon, Sharpest) that was 121 draw calls instead of 1,100 on the valley floor, and nearby terrain's work on the render thread dropped from 0.94 ms to 0.65 ms a frame; the frame rate stayed the same there, because the graphics card was the limit. The picture is the same. It costs about 40% more graphics memory for nearby terrain. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.regions=false`.
- Cave culling comes from studying Horizon, an open-source Rust rewrite of Minecraft, and was written from scratch for Earth.
- The far view distance now changes while you play. Before, moving the far view slider on the pause menu (or the distance setting) only took effect after restarting the game. Now the far terrain grows or shrinks when you let go of the slider: the haze, the horizon's edge and what a shader pack is told about the distance all follow at once, and the new ground fills in as it does when you travel.
- New optional setting, a frame rate to hold (`targetFrameRate` in the client settings, also "Hold frame rate" on Sodium's Earth page; off by default). When the game runs slower than the rate you set, far terrain sharpness steps down one choice at a time, never below Fast. When there is room again it steps back up, never past the sharpness you chose. It leaves the far distance alone. Each step up is a trial, and a trial that had to be taken back makes the next one wait twice as long, so a computer that can't hold the next choice settles instead of switching back and forth. F3 shows when it is holding the sharpness down. It has run in unit tests and on a software renderer only, not yet on a real graphics card.
- The spawn picker and `/earth where` now say how stretched the map is. An Earth world is a Mercator map with blocks 1 m wide at the world's home latitude, so land nearer the poles is drawn wider than in life while heights stay real (Denali from a Yosemite home is 1.75 times as wide). The Create World screens say how much the map stretches away from the spot you pick, the travel map and a server's spawn picker say how stretched the spot under the pin is, and `/earth where` says it for where you stand. Nothing about the terrain changes.
- Shader packs that support Distant Horizons but not Voxy now light far terrain. Of the 60 most downloaded Iris packs on Modrinth, 22 are like that, among them Bliss, Rethinking Voxels and Super Duper Vanilla. Before, far terrain under those packs was drawn in plain colors the pack knew nothing about, so it came out washed out or dark and without the pack's light, shadows and fog. Now Earth draws far terrain with the pack's own Distant Horizons terrain program and hands the pack far terrain's depth where it expects Distant Horizons', so the pack shades far hills as it would shade Distant Horizons' terrain. Distant Horizons itself is not needed and should not be installed. Under these packs far terrain shows each block's average color rather than its texture, as Distant Horizons does. Packs with Voxy support, such as Photon and Complementary, are drawn as before. Off with `-Dearthplatform.far.dhPacks=false`.
- Find any town by name. The globe now has a search box: type a town or a famous place, press Enter, and the pin lands there, on the Create World globe, the travel map and the survival map. `/earth visit Zermatt` or `/earth visit Paris, Texas` takes you to where a town is, and `/earth find <name>` lists every match to click. Before, there were 29 famous places and everywhere else needed coordinates. The list is every town of 1,000 people or more (about 172,000, from GeoNames), carried inside the mod, so nothing is looked up online. The Earth is still wild land: you arrive at the town's spot, not in a town. The jar grows from about 4 MB to about 7 MB.
- Place codes. `/earth code` gives twelve letters and digits, such as `S6GV-H1C2-DS2C`, that hold where you stand to about a meter and which way you face. Anyone who may travel types `/earth code` and the code to stand on the same spot, facing the same way. A mistyped code is refused rather than sending you somewhere else.
- Postcards. Press **F8** and Earth saves a screenshot with no hotbar, hand or chat, and the place, its coordinates, its height and its place code written along the bottom, as `earth-postcard-<date>.png` in the `screenshots` folder. Codes and postcards are not given out during a Where on Earth round.
- One-click install packs. Each release now comes with `earth-platform-neoforge-<build>.mrpack` and `earth-platform-fabric-<build>.mrpack`. Open one in the Modrinth App, Prism Launcher or ATLauncher and it sets up Minecraft 1.21.1, the mod loader, Earth Platform, Sodium, Iris and Photon, with Photon switched on. You still set the memory to 6 GB yourself. See docs/INSTALL.md.
