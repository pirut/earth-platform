Earth Platform build 70: Minecraft's own caves in new worlds, nearby blocks hidden behind rock no longer drawn, and far terrain does less work on your processor while you fly.

Needs Minecraft 1.21.1 and either NeoForge 21.1.256 or newer, or Fabric Loader 0.16.14 or newer with Fabric API. For shaders, add Iris 1.8 and Sodium 0.8 and a Voxy-capable pack such as Photon v1.3b. Give the game about 6 GB of memory with shaders.

New since build 69:
- New worlds carve their caves with Minecraft's own cave code: cheese caverns with stone pillars, winding spaghetti tunnels, thin noodle caves and cave entrances, the same shapes and sizes as a vanilla world, worked out by the same density functions and noises vanilla uses, seeded as vanilla seeds them. Before, Earth had its own simpler caves: smooth blobs and tunnels about 10 blocks wide, everywhere at every depth, so caves felt too big and too samey. The caves follow the ground as the rock layers do, so they sit at vanilla's depths under each valley or seabed. Worlds made before build 70 keep their old caves, so new ground meets saved ground without cut-off caves. Underground cubes take about 0.3 ms more each to make.
- Flying no longer makes Earth recheck every far column each frame. Before, each time the camera moved or turned even slightly, Earth worked out again which of tens of thousands of far columns were in view, and queued them all to draw. Now it picks the columns that could show from anywhere within 16 blocks of the camera, in a slightly wider view, and keeps that pick while you stay inside it. Columns that can't be seen draw nothing, so the picture is the same. On our test PC (RTX 2080 Ti, Photon, Sharpest settings, flying at the Yosemite vista) far terrain's work on the render thread dropped from about 4.1 ms to 1.6 ms a frame.
- Far lakes, rivers and sea are drawn in large batches instead of one far column at a time, in each of the water passes. Far water's work on the render thread dropped from about 1.2 ms to 0.9 ms a frame on the same test.
- With a shader pack, nearby terrain that can't cast a shadow into your view is left out of the shadow map, as Iris already does for Minecraft's own chunks (and only where the pack allows it). Under Photon on the Yosemite valley floor the shadow map drew 211 nearby cubes instead of 1,335. The picture is the same. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.shadowCull=false`.
- Nearby blocks hidden behind rock are no longer drawn. Each nearby cube now notes which of its sides its open space joins, and Earth draws only the cubes the camera could see into through that open space, the way Minecraft culls its own chunk sections (it never did this for Earth's cubes before). Standing on the Yosemite valley floor, it draws 74 nearby cubes instead of 211; in a cave room, 155 instead of 338. Chests, signs and other block entities in those hidden cubes are skipped too (a beacon's beam still shows). The picture is the same. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.caveCull=false`.
- Of the nearby cubes still drawn, those hidden behind other terrain are now checked on the graphics card first: the cubes seen last frame draw first, then each other cube draws only if a box around it would show past what is already drawn. The card decides in the same frame, so nothing pops in late, and the picture is the same. It uses OpenGL 3.3, so it works on Macs too. Off with `-Dearthplatform.cubes.occlusion=false`.
- Under a shader pack, far terrain's depth pre-pass now draws the far terrain seen last frame first, then only the far terrain that would still show, so far hills hidden behind nearer ones skip that pass too. Needs OpenGL 4.3, so not on Macs. Off with `-Dearthplatform.far.twoPhase=false`.
- These three ideas come from studying Horizon, an open-source Rust rewrite of Minecraft, and were written from scratch for Earth.
- On that PC the frame rate stayed the same, about 78 fps standing and flying, because the graphics card was the limit there. Computers with a slower processor, or playing without shaders, should see more of the saving.
- Both can be switched off with `-Dearthplatform.far.viewMargin=false` and `-Dearthplatform.far.batchWater=false` (see docs/FLAGS.md).
- The horizon now shows within seconds of arriving somewhere new. Before, the two coarsest far levels (the land beyond about 23 km, out to the edge of view) were the last to appear, because each of their columns reads the most map data: on a first visit to the Matterhorn the horizon took 30 to 45 seconds to fill in. Now the server first makes a quick stand-in for each of those columns from coarse copies of the same data (elevation and land cover at that level's own size, and every 32nd row of the climate grids), with the same surface rules, and sends those first. The game shows them and still asks for the real columns, which replace the stand-ins as they arrive. In the same test the horizon was on screen 2 seconds after arriving. A stand-in takes about 1 to 2 ms to make; against the real columns their heights differ by 0.1 to 0.5 blocks on average and the top block is the same in 86 to 98% of places, so the swap is hard to see from that far away. The real columns are exactly what they were before (same fingerprints with stand-ins on and off), and finish about 2 seconds later than before. Works the same with or without a shader pack, since stand-ins are ordinary far columns. Server and client must both run build 70 (the network version changed). Off with `-Dearthplatform.far.previews=false` on the server or the client.
- We measured a ready-made file of the whole planet's horizon first, and it is not workable: about 20 to 70 GB, months of processor time to make, and a new file for every world, because each world's scale depends on its home latitude.
- The whole far view now shows within seconds of arriving somewhere new, not only the horizon. Every far level gets the quick stand-ins the two coarsest got: each ring is filled at its own detail from the little map data that level needs, then sharpens in place as the full terrain is made, with no change to the finished terrain. Forests stand as block crowns until their trees are drawn one by one. On our four-core test machine the view in front was fully covered 5 seconds after arriving at a place seen before and 7 seconds after a first visit, where its full terrain took 30 and 90 seconds. `-Dearthplatform.far.previewLevel=7` keeps the stand-ins to the horizon as before.
- Zooming in sharpens what you look at first. The columns a spyglass or zoom mod looks at were asked for after everything else the far view was still loading, and only at the next half-second plan. Now they are asked for the moment the view zooms, ahead of the rest, and get stand-ins too, so a zoomed mountain sharpens in about a second and then settles.
- Far terrain fills in with nothing left hanging. Stand-ins are asked for and built in one order: what you look at first, a coarse backdrop of the whole view, then each ring of detail from the horizon in, while the real terrain comes from you outward, so the two meet in the middle and the ground at your feet never waits behind stand-ins. At low frame rates the far mesh workers also get as much work as they can finish in a frame; they stood idle between frames before.
- Stand-ins now wear the same rock as the terrain that replaces them. Volcanic cones (Shasta, Rainier, Fuji) were left out of stand-ins, so a whole mountain showed andesite and gravel and then turned to tuff and basalt as the real columns came. Stand-ins now find cones on the coarse tiles they already hold: on Mount Shasta the top block matches the real column in 97 to 99.8% of cells at every level, where the fine levels matched in 21 to 28%. The ground right round you also keeps half the far workers while stand-ins are made, and its meshes go first.
- The ground round you comes first again. Timed on an M4 Pro Mac, asking for every level's stand-ins at once kept the ground at your feet a plain of coarse slabs for ten seconds on a first visit, where it stood whole in three before, and made the travel itself three seconds slower: their downloads stood in the way of the landing's. Now only the coarse backdrop is fetched ahead of a travel, the two finest levels have no stand-ins at all (their real columns are the first to arrive), and the levels between get theirs once the ground round you is in.
- Far terrain uses more of a big processor: half the cores less one make far columns (five on a 12-core machine, eleven on 24), where it was at most eight. Four-core machines are unchanged. Set with `-Dearthplatform.far.threads`.
- Land cover lookups do less work each, which all terrain making shares.
- The idea of filling every ring at its own detail first and refining after comes from how SeedGen by MobiusMeansBusiness fills Voxy's and Distant Horizons' levels from the world seed; ours is written from scratch for Earth's map data.
