# Developer flags

Earth Platform reads a few Java system properties, given as JVM arguments such as `-Dearthplatform.far.threads=4`.
Players never need them: the settings files and menus cover everything a player changes (see [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md#settings)).
They are for testing, measuring and comparing against older behavior.
The experimental flying, cloud and far terrain flags are explained together in [FLIGHT-RENDERING.md](FLIGHT-RENDERING.md).

Most are read once when the class that uses them loads, so set them on the command line, not at runtime.
The frame rate to hold (`targetFrameRate` in the client settings file, off by default) is a setting, not a flag: see [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md#settings). Seven of them
(`far.detail`, `far.textured`, `clouds.layered`, `clouds.quality`, `clouds.opacity`, `earth.offline`, `earth.imagery`) are also set by the settings files through
`EarthConfig`; a value given on the command line wins over the file. The three cloud flags are read every frame.

Every other on/off flag in the tables below is also a toggle in the settings files and on the config screen (Mods → Earth Platform → Config), under the
group named in [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md#engine-switches), with its default as the setting's default. A toggle at its default changes nothing, and a value given on the command line wins over the file.
Flags that are not on or off (counts, distances, `auto` modes: `far.threads`, `far.store`, `far.depthPrepass`, `photon.compat`, `earth.transfers`, `earth.home` and the like) stay command-line only.
So do the six elevation layer flags (`earth.copernicus`, `earth.3dep`, `earth.hrdem`, `earth.rema`, `earth.ign`, `earth.gsi`): they are not settings because the bare-earth heights every world is read through are not yet tied to each world's own pin (see the note under World data), so a setting could not keep existing worlds' layers.
`EngineToggles` lists the toggles; the settings files are read by `EngineToggles.preload` first thing when the mod starts, before the classes that read these flags once can load, so a changed toggle takes effect the next time the game starts.
The three `earth.*` flags for canopy, maps and realms are read when a world is made and pinned in it, so they take effect for the next new world at once. `EngineTogglesTest` checks that each on/off flag in the code has a toggle (or a stated reason it has none), with the default shown here.

A world pins its elevation layers when it is made, but the bare-earth ground (`BareEarth`, which `EarthGenerator` reads every height through) is one object per game built from the layer flags as they are, not from each world's own pin. So a layer flag affects the new ground of existing worlds too, with a seam against what they saved. Tying bare earth to the pin is future work, and until then the six layer flags are command-line only, not settings.

| `earthplatform.cubes.skipInterior` | `false` | Meshing a near cube skips a cube of solid stone or deepslate sealed in by more of it on all six faces, below its column's sky, with no block entities and only vanilla's plain stone models: it is neither lit nor walked over, since every block in it is buried, its light is dark and its air joins no face. `cubes.skipBuried` already leaves its blocks out of the model pass one by one; this also saves the light, the walk and the cave graph. | `earthplatform.cubes.reuseSnapshots` | `false` | A near cube's blocks are copied once for all the mesh snapshots it is in (up to 27), kept for the last 1,024 cubes, instead of once for each. | `earthplatform.cubes.checkMesh` | `false` | Also meshes and lights every near cube the plain way (`cubes.skipBuried`, `cubes.reusePose`, `cubes.plainRandom`, `cubes.fastLight` and `cubes.skipInterior` off) and logs a warning naming any cube whose light or vertices differ, with a count every 1,000 cubes, for testing them. Under a shader pack, vertex normals are counted apart: Iris works a quad's normal out again only while the render thread is drawing the level, so a mesh made twice can differ there either way | `CubeMesher` |
| `earthplatform.cubes.caveCull` | on | Cave culling, as vanilla culls its sections: each near cube's mesh notes which of its faces its air joins, and a search from the camera's cube steps into a neighbour only through a face the cube it is in joins to the face it came in by, never back toward the camera. Cubes it never reaches lie behind rock from every point of the camera's cube and are not drawn in the view (the shadow map still draws them). Cubes not loaded or not meshed yet count as open, and nothing is culled while the camera is inside a solid block (a spectator in rock), as vanilla does. `false` draws every cube in the frustum, for comparing. | `earthplatform.cubes.caveLine` | on | With `cubes.caveCull`, past 60 blocks a step is also taken only where the line from the cube back to the camera runs through cubes the search reached, as vanilla does; `false` leaves that look out | `CubeCaves` |
| `earthplatform.cubes.occlusion` | off | Occlusion culling of near cubes in two phases, with OpenGL 3.3 occlusion queries and conditional rendering (so on a Mac too): first the cubes whose box was seen when last tested, then each other cube's box (grown by a block) against the depth they left, and that cube's layers drawn on condition that some of its box passed, decided by the GPU in the same frame. The same pixels, nothing a frame late. A quarter of the cubes drawn first are tested again each frame. Only in a frame's first pass of the level, never in the shadow map. Off by default: on a 2080 Ti under Photon the tests cost more than they saved (valley floor 64 fps with them, 73 without). `true` turns it on | `CubeOcclusion`, `CubeRenderer` |
| `earthplatform.cubes.occlusionFront` | on | The occlusion test draws only the sides of each box facing the camera, which lie in front of everything in it: the same answer for half the fill. `false` draws both sides | `CubeOcclusion` |
| `earthplatform.cubes.shadowCull` | on | Under a shader pack, near cubes go into the shadow map through Iris's own shadow frustum, the one Iris culls its sections by: what can cast no shadow into the view is left out where the pack allows (`shadow.culling`), and a pack that needs every caster gets them all. | `earthplatform.cubes.shadowCaves` | on | With `cubes.shadowCull` and `cubes.caveCull`: where the cave search shows the camera shut in (nothing it reached touches the edge of the near cubes, so the view never leaves them), the shadow map takes only the cubes whose shadow, falling away from the light, meets the box of what the search reached (grown 16 blocks). | `earthplatform.cubes.regions` | on | Near cube meshes share vertex buffers by region (8 cubes a side), their vertices placed in the region, and each opaque layer is drawn with one `glMultiDrawElementsBaseVertex` a buffer, in the main pass and the shadow map; translucent layers keep their exact back-to-front order, drawn in runs of cubes that share a buffer. Buffers are of fixed size and meshes never move, so nothing is copied on the GPU (OpenGL 3.2, a Mac too); they take about 1.4 times the meshes' own bytes. Cubes drawn on condition of an occlusion query still draw one by one. The same picture bar a few edge pixels in a hundred thousand, from rounding a vertex's place in the region instead of in the cube. `false` gives every cube its own buffers and draws, as before | `CubeRegions`, `CubeRanges`, `CubeRenderer`, `CubeMesher` |
| `earthplatform.cubic.lightmapRing` | on | In cubic worlds without a shader pack, uploads the game's lightmap to three textures in turn, so an upload never waits on frames still drawing with the last one; the same pixels are drawn. `false` always uploads to the one texture, for comparing | `CubicLightmapTexture` |
